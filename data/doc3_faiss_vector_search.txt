Vector Search with FAISS

FAISS is a library for efficient similarity search over dense vectors. At small to medium scale,
an exact search index such as IndexFlatL2 or IndexFlatIP simply compares a query vector against
every stored vector and returns the closest matches, guaranteeing exact nearest neighbors at the
cost of scanning the entire index for every query. This is usually fine for collections of a few
hundred thousand vectors or fewer.

At larger scale, exact search becomes too slow, and approximate methods trade a small amount
of accuracy for large speed gains. IVF (inverted file) indexes partition the vector space into
clusters using k-means, and at query time only a subset of clusters closest to the query are
searched, rather than the entire collection. HNSW (hierarchical navigable small world) indexes
build a graph structure that allows a query to navigate toward its nearest neighbors without
visiting most of the index.

Choosing between inner product and L2 distance depends on whether the embeddings are
normalized. If vectors are normalized to unit length, ranking by inner product is equivalent to
ranking by cosine similarity, and is often preferred for text embeddings because it focuses on
the direction of the vector rather than its magnitude.

Index quality should be validated the same way any retrieval system is validated: by measuring
whether known relevant items are actually returned in the top-k results for representative
queries, not by inspecting the index structure alone.
