Prompt Injection in Retrieval-Augmented Systems

When a language model's context window includes text retrieved from an external source, that
retrieved text can contain instructions rather than just information. Prompt injection occurs
when a malicious or unexpected document contains language crafted to redirect the model's
behavior, for example instructing it to ignore its original task or reveal system-level
instructions. This is a distinct risk from adversarial perturbations on model inputs, because
the attack is expressed in natural language rather than in pixel or token-level noise.

RAG systems are particularly exposed to this risk because retrieved documents are, by design,
inserted into the model's context alongside the user's query, often without the same scrutiny
applied to the query itself. A document scraped from an untrusted source, uploaded by another
user, or pulled from the open web can carry embedded instructions that the retrieval step has
no way of filtering out, since retrieval is optimized for topical relevance, not for safety.

Mitigations include clearly delimiting retrieved content from instructions in the prompt
template, training or prompting the model to treat retrieved text as data rather than as
commands, and running a separate classifier over retrieved chunks to flag suspicious
instruction-like content before it reaches the generation step. None of these mitigations are
complete on their own, which is why evaluation harnesses for RAG systems increasingly include
adversarially constructed documents designed specifically to test whether injected instructions
are followed.
